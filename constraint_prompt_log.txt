In your system, they mainly appear where edge cases, constraints, and acceptance criteria overlap. Here are the main kinds of tie situations you can conceptually identify.

1. Ordering of simultaneous registration requests

Your constraints require deterministic ordering and the waitlist to follow FIFO behavior. Edge cases such as multiple users attempting to register when only one slot remains create a situation where more than one valid outcome could exist. A tie-breaker is needed to decide which user receives the final slot and which users are placed on the waitlist while still satisfying the ordering constraint.

2. Waitlist promotion after cancellation

When a registered user cancels and multiple users are waiting, the system must choose which waitlisted user is promoted. The edge case here is that several users may be eligible at the same time. The tie-breaker ensures the promotion follows the constraint that the waitlist ordering remains stable and predictable.

3. Repeated or rapid user operations

Mo’s persona introduces cases where users frequently cancel or re-register. If multiple actions occur close together, the system could potentially process them in different orders and still satisfy the functional requirements. A tie-breaker ensures that operation processing remains deterministic, so the same sequence of actions always leads to the same outcome.

4. Handling duplicate or conflicting requests

Some edge cases involve duplicate registration attempts or cancelling users that may or may not exist in the system. A tie-breaker conceptually ensures that conflicting operations are resolved consistently, rather than leaving the system in an ambiguous state or producing inconsistent results.}
